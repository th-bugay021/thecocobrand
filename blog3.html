Why We Started The Coco Brand

📅 February 2025 ⏱ 4 min read

The Coco Brand did not start in a boardroom or a factory. It started with frustration — the kind that comes from spending money on lip colour after lip colour, only to find that nothing looked right on your skin.

The Problem We Could Not Ignore

For years, the beauty industry told women with deeper skin tones that "this red is universal" or "this nude works for everyone." But anyone with melanin-rich skin knows the truth — most mainstream lip colours oxidise to muddy tones, nudes wash out to ashy grey, and bolds look flat rather than vibrant.

We were tired of the workarounds. Tired of mixing products, layering formulas, or settling for close enough. We wanted lip colours that were made for us — not adapted from formulas built for someone else.

The Beginning

What started as a personal project quickly became something bigger. After months of testing, sampling, and reformulating, we created our first three shades — each one specifically calibrated to look stunning on deeper skin tones without losing vibrancy or true colour payoff.

We tested on real women. We listened to feedback. We reformulated again. And when we finally had something we were proud of, we knew it was ready.

We did not want to make lipstick. We wanted to make confidence — in a bullet, in a shade, in a formula that finally showed up the way we deserved.

What Coco Means to Us

The name Coco is warmth. It is richness. It is the depth of colour that has always lived in our culture — in our fashion, our art, our celebrations. We built The Coco Brand to reflect that richness back to the women who deserved to see themselves in their beauty products.

Every shade we release goes through rigorous testing across a wide spectrum of skin tones. Every formula is clean, cruelty free, and built to perform.

Where We Are Going

This is just the beginning. We are expanding our shade range, developing new formulas, and building a community of women who celebrate bold, unapologetic beauty. The Coco Brand is more than a lipstick — it is a statement. And we are just getting started.
